The pipeline at a glance

Consent files are the closest early signal of new lot supply. One consent file usually means one proposed new lot, though some are lot additions or easements.

Filed in the last 30 days

    Water and sewer capacity

    Only matters in the serviced towns. Nearly all Muskoka Lakes waterfront applications above are on private septic and wells.

    Where it stands now

    • The District runs 9 water and 8 wastewater systems, each with finite capacity, and says capacity in several of them is becoming constrained.
    • On August 20, 2026 District Council approved a district-wide conditional capacity process (Report 11(2026)-2). Developers in constrained areas can proceed at their own risk while upgrades are built, but cannot connect or occupy until capacity is confirmed.
    • The model came from Huntsville's Coveside wastewater catchment, where the District says upgrades have now freed capacity for projects to connect.
    • The formal by-law amendment is due back to Council in Q1 2027.

    How it's built

    • Every register row comes from a public notice posted by the municipality and links to its source (notice PDF, notice page or the Township's document library). Bracebridge only keeps recent notices on its site, so its earlier summer notices may be missing.
    • "Meeting" is the hearing date on the notice. A past date means the hearing was held, not that a decision was made.
    • Huntsville uses a Community Planning Permit system instead of a zoning by-law, so its "permit amendments" are the equivalent of rezonings.
    • Bracebridge, Gravenhurst, Lake of Bays and Georgian Bay planning comes from the District of Muskoka's PlanHub feed: subdivisions, condominiums and District Official Plan amendments it approves, plus local rezonings, consents and Official Plan amendments it reviews. Their minor variances are mostly not in it, except Lake of Bays planning permit variances and Gravenhurst variances from the Town's own public records.
    • Addresses and assessed values come from the District's civic address and assessment parcel layers, matched by roll number.
    • Building permits come from the Township of Muskoka Lakes permit layer on the District GIS, January 2025 to October 8, 2026, grouped by roll number. A planning file and a permit on the same roll number are linked automatically.
    • The map draws Muskoka Lakes applications from the Township's planning layer on the District GIS (2,362 applications since 1999; this map shows 2025 onward), lakes from the District's waterbody layer, and township lines from its boundary layer.
